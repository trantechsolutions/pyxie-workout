# Eight lines, 248 names, one egg

A note on why Pyxie just got harder to predict.

---

When I started Pyxie, three was the right number.

Three elemental lines — Ember, Tide, Verdant — felt like enough to make the blind-box hatch real. Three is enough variance for "you don't know what's inside" to mean something the first time you crack an egg. Three keeps the art workload survivable for a solo build. Three is the safe answer.

But three is also the number every gacha game starts with. Three is "fire/water/grass." Three is a tutorial.

So we added five more.

---

## What changed

The roster expanded from three baseline lines to eight: **Ember, Tide, Verdant, Gale, Stone, Umbra, Aurora, Static.** Each line keeps the same binary-branching evolution tree it always had — every pet still chooses between a primary and an alt-form at each stage, driven by the actual workouts you've logged — so per-line, you still get 31 nodes across 5 stages.

Eight lines × 31 nodes = **248 named creatures.**

If you do the math: that's 128 final-stage forms alone. One playthrough sees maybe four or five of those. A dedicated player across a year of pets might meet thirty. The rest live in the catalog and never come out of the egg.

That's the point.

---

## Why expand at all

The honest answer: the original three felt like a sample size. Once a player saw Drakorath, Leviathos, and Sylvadrake, they'd seen the silhouette of every line. The "you don't know what's inside" promise weakened with each hatch.

Eight lines re-opens the question. After your fifth playthrough, you've still never met Umbra. You've never seen what Aurora's stage-4 leaves look like. You don't know yet that Static exists.

The egg is more honest about not telling you what's coming because there's genuinely more it isn't telling you.

---

## What didn't change

- **You still don't pick the line.** The egg rolls. Whatever hatches is yours for that pet's life.
- **Permadeath is still permadeath.** Skip two days, the pet's in critical. A few more, it's gone. No revives, no second chances, no buying your way back. Bigger roster, same rules.
- **Your training still picks the branch.** Easy walks grow one creature. Hard sessions grow a different one. The line is decided for you; the path is yours.
- **The blind-box hatch is still the first moment.** A speckled egg. Hints of every line, commitment to none. Now it speckles in eight colors instead of five.

---

## The five new lines, in one line each

- **Gale** — *carried on the wind.* Mobility, speed, jump complexity. Lithe silhouettes; pale silver palette.
- **Stone** — *patient and unbroken.* Isometrics, holds, slow strength. Earthy and chunky; gritty sand-and-clay palette.
- **Umbra** — *shaped in shadow.* Late-night training, low-light flavor. Deep violets and bruised blacks.
- **Aurora** — *lit from within.* Recovery and restorative work. Pastel pinks and soft golds.
- **Static** — *charged and restless.* High-frequency intervals. Yellow on yellow on yellow.

Each comes with its own evolution tree — 31 nodes, every node named. None of them are "more powerful" than the elder three. Pyxie has no power curve. Pets just grow, branch, and eventually die.

---

## On the catalog as a horizon

A few playtesters asked: should we ship a public dex showing every form?

The answer is yes — but not the way Pokémon does it.

Pokémon's National Dex is a *checklist*. You're supposed to fill it. Trade for what you can't catch. Eventually own them all.

Pyxie's roster is the opposite. It's a *horizon* — the boundary of what could happen, most of which won't. You can't trade for a creature you haven't earned. You can't reroll a line you didn't get. Most of the catalog is meant to be seen and never owned. The catalog exists to make your one pet feel like *one of 248*, not like *the only thing in the game.*

We're publishing the full roster alongside this post — every line, every stage, every name — so you can see what you're not getting. That's part of the deal.

## The Eevee debt

I've said this before in the founder note on the landing page, but it's worth saying again here:

The branching evolution mechanic is a love letter to Eevee. The Pokémon with eight different futures depending on how you raised it was the single most interesting design idea in a franchise that had hundreds. The fact that one starting point could fork into wildly different creatures based on your choices — not your team composition, not your money, but the actual texture of how you played — that's the design idea Pyxie is built on.

Eight elemental lines is, deliberately, the same number as Eevee's evolutions. That's not coincidence. It's tribute.

The Eevee fork was one creature with eight futures. Pyxie inverts it: eight creatures, 248 futures, one egg per playthrough.

---

## What's next

We're not adding line nine. Probably ever.

The eight here are deliberate — five elemental archetypes (fire/water/earth/wind/stone) plus three texture lines (shadow/light/electric) that cover the moods the elements alone don't. Adding a ninth would dilute the speckle in the egg and require an art lift roughly equal to the entire expansion we just shipped. Eight is enough.

What's next is the rest of the product. Apple Health sync. Smartwatch integration. A wiki you can spoil yourself with if you decide you can't stand the suspense.

But the roster, for now, is locked.
